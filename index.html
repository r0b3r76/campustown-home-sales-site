Data & methods

How these numbers were made

The dataset

One row per completed home sale, 35,442 rows, collected in March 2026 from public property listings. Sales closed between March 27, 2023 and March 25, 2026, so 2023 and 2026 are partial years. Every number on this page is calculated in your browser from data/site_data.json, a compact copy of data/college_town_home_sales.csv made by scripts/build_site_data.py. Nothing is estimated or typed in by hand.

Rules

  • Price rule. Analyses use sales with a sale price from $10,000 to $5,000,000, inclusive, which keeps 35,371 of 35,442 rows. This removes clear errors (40 sales under $10,000, one at $0) and 31 sales over $5,000,000, up to $81,510,000. Q2 and Q3 use all rows on purpose, and Q9 counts all rows with a sale date.
  • Days on market is the contract date minus the list date, in days, using only the 28,042 rows that have both dates. A blank date is never treated as zero. The closing date is not used, because it adds about a month of closing time.
  • Medians are used throughout unless a question asks for a mean, because a few very large sales pull means far above typical prices. With an even number of sales, the median is the average of the middle two.
  • Campus area is the university column (11 values), assigned from each home's coordinates. City is city and state together (18 values). Year and month come from the sale date.
  • Missing values. Blank basement and fireplace values are shown as Unknown, not No. Living area, price per square foot and the living-area filter skip rows with no living area or a living area of 0 (127 rows are 0). Q6 uses rows with an annual tax greater than $0 and below 10% of the sale price, which drops obvious errors such as a $1,000,000,000 tax bill.
  • No deduplication by address. listing_id is the key. 66 rows share a street address, mostly separate condo units in State College, and all are kept.
  • Ranges include their end values, so a $400,000 maximum includes a $400,000 sale. When cities or campus areas tie, all of them are reported.
  • Rounding. Prices are shown in whole dollars, and other numbers to one decimal place (two for tax rates).

Charts

The ten questions use eight chart types: a bar chart (Q1, Q7), a dumbbell chart (Q2), a slope chart (Q3), a diverging bar chart (Q4), a box plot (Q5), a lollipop chart (Q6, Q8), a column chart (Q9) and a histogram (Q10). Every chart has a tooltip and a "View the numbers" table, except Q10, whose two numbers are in the written answer.

Map

Each campus area and city is placed at the median latitude and longitude of its sales. Map tiles are from CARTO and OpenStreetMap contributors.
